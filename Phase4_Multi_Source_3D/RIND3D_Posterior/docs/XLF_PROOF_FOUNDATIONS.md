# XLF proof foundations

**PROVEN (abstract).** A finite run can certify its output from lower volumes of feasible and infeasible regions and the fixed world capacity. Search order, termination on other inputs, and discovery of every component are unnecessary for soundness. **CONDITIONAL (software).** The theorem applies to the numerical program only if it earns those volume credits legitimately and binds its error bound to the decoded output. The [transition design](XLF_CORRECT_BY_CONSTRUCTION.md) gives one way to enforce the hypotheses. The [implementation audit](XLF_CHECKER_FREE_FEASIBILITY.md) identifies which hypotheses are still open.

## Input and target

Fix one validated geometry, window and source-specific response channel. Let Ω = [0,1]³, with Lebesgue volume μ. The canonical continuous solid K is a finite union of bounded closed full-dimensional convex polytopes strictly inside Ω. The canonical decoder interprets the binary64 parameters as exact rationals. Retain the resulting rational defining rows. Let D be the open world outside the **closed** continuous window box and at Euclidean distance strictly greater than 2⁻²⁶ from K. This defines source legality; the success set of any particular clearance test need not exhaust D.

Receivers are the specified voxel centres (o+i+½)/1024. A valid input's SOLID mask agrees with K at those centres. For each FREE receiver r, the observed bit Y(r) is active iff the closed segment [s,r] misses K. Tangencies and endpoint contact block. Define F as the sources in D reproducing **every** FREE bit. Distinct channels get distinct conditional targets. Sorting a heterogeneous block into two classes must account for every free receiver. The theorem can also use a versioned authoritative validated observation decoder as a trusted input.

For measurable F with μ(F)>0 the target is the pushforward of uniform volume on F onto half-open 128³ cells, with any consistent outer-face ownership. Thus qᵢ = μ(F∩cellᵢ)/μ(F). This is conditional on the supplied full world and the declared volume measure. An inference model that sees only the local observation has uncertainty over worlds as well. A generated source witness alone does not prove μ(F)>0.

## What local and collective evidence can prove

**PROVEN — inverse shadow without adjacency or generic position.** Write one body's rows as affine functions fᵢ≤0. For a FREE receiver r put aᵢ=fᵢ(r), bᵢ=fᵢ(s). The segment hits this body iff there exists u≥0 such that bᵢ+u aᵢ≤0 for every i. Indeed, (s+ur)/(1+u) parameterizes the closed segment except r, and r is outside the body. At least one aᵢ is positive. Eliminating the scalar u gives exactly

* bᵢ≤0 whenever aᵢ≥0;
* aᵢ bⱼ−aⱼ bᵢ≤0 for every aᵢ>0 and aⱼ<0.

The first test enforces nonnegative upper endpoints and the zero-slope rows. The second states that every lower endpoint −bⱼ/aⱼ is below every upper endpoint −bᵢ/aᵢ. Choose the largest lower endpoint, including zero, to prove sufficiency. Equal endpoints are admitted. If r lies in the body, every segment hits by endpoint contact. This all-pairs formulation supplies a simple exact specification for faster silhouette-adjacency filters. The inverse shadow C(r) is a closed convex polyhedron. Reciprocity follows from [r,s]=[s,r]. With finite receivers, F is measurable as a finite Boolean combination of these sets and D.

**PROVEN — two collective rules.** Let Q and T be bounded convex polytopes enclosing a source region and some receivers. For one fixed convex blocker, every segment from Q to T hits iff all pairs consisting of one vertex of Q and one vertex of T hit. Fix a receiver vertex, use convexity of its inverse shadow in s, then fix s and apply the same convexity in the receiver coordinate. If an endpoint is inside the blocker, its shadow is the whole space. For visibility, the union of *all* segments from Q to T equals conv(Q ∪ T). In a convex combination collect the Q and T weights and divide by their respective sums; zero total on one side gives an endpoint. Consequently, strict separation of this hull from each body proves visibility for the entire product. Empty intersection with every body is also necessary for visibility of the continuous product. Using the hull of **sampled** receivers is a sufficient relaxation; a failed hull test leaves the sampled question open.

For example a common separating affine form can be checked by bounds on Q, T and the body. In an H-description a nonnegative combination of active rows, with a verified constant offset, suffices to prove a proposed inequality. A faster search may propose the separator without being trusted to find one. A DARK proof needs only one fixed blocker on the product. It is sufficient but not necessary in a union of bodies. In particular, different blockers at all vertex pairs do not justify extending their labels by convexity. The executable counterexample has two individually blocked endpoint sources and a visible midpoint.

**PROVEN — an alternative collective rule for multiple blockers.** Let H be an open cavity whose entire boundary is contained in the closed solid union. If an active receiver r lies in H, then every feasible source belongs to H. Any continuous segment from outside H to r has a boundary crossing (including a source endpoint on the boundary), which blocks the segment. Different sources can cross completely different obstacles. The certificate needs a proof of boundary coverage and one active observation. If H is contained in one posterior cell and a positive-volume feasible subset is independently certified against the whole window, the grid posterior is exactly that cell's unit mass. A gap in the boundary invalidates this argument. A merely local SOLID sample cannot establish boundary coverage in continuous geometry.

**PROVEN — necessary interface condition.** For an adjacent active/inactive pair l,k the entire segment [l,k] must be verified free for this inference. Fix any feasible s. The receiver shadow of the finite closed solid is closed: take a convergent sequence of receivers and a convergent subsequence of intersection parameters in [0,1]. There is a first shadowed p on [l,k]. No interior point of the solid can be present on [s,p], since an interior intersection would persist for nearby receivers; both endpoints are free. For a polytope a contact is on an edge or vertex, or in a facet interior. A transverse facet-interior crossing enters the body. A coplanar contact must reach a facet boundary on the way to the exterior endpoint p. At a vertex choose an incident edge. In every case there is a finite obstacle edge intersecting conv{s,l,k}. This weaker edge-incidence result already proves a safe filter when **all** edges are admitted. The stronger silhouette-edge restriction in Proposition A′ additionally uses the incident-facet argument at an edge or the cyclic vertex figure at a vertex. For any set of verified interfaces, intersect their **unions** of eligible explanations. Removing an explanation requires its own proof. A positive product of two facet evaluations at both endpoints does not imply their product is positive throughout; testing both affine factors strictly on the same side throughout is sufficient. See [Claude's proposition and degeneracies](../../../../G2-02-xlf-math/Phase4_Multi_Source_3D/RIND3D_Posterior/docs/LOCALFIRST_E123.md).

**PROVEN — compression under explicit hypotheses.** On Q, discharge any redundant halfspace by a valid implication. If a dark observation has one remaining possible blocker, its condition is the intersection of that blocker's active halfspaces. A lit observation excludes each possible blocker, usually giving disjunctions. A lens of fitted planes cannot replace those disjunctions. For closed inequalities hᵢ≤0 in one oriented plane pencil, if every omitted affine row is a nonnegative linear combination of the retained rows (including the constant coordinate), all omitted inequalities follow. The analogous strict conclusion requires a positive coefficient on a strict inequality. Two extreme rows suffice when they generate the relevant pointed coefficient cone. Antipodal or wrapped directions, a vanishing row and changing blockers require separate handling. The search may instead suggest any cut and recertify both sides independently. No compression claim then enters the trusted proof.

The finite constraints specify F exactly as a set expression. A bounded computation usually determines just part of that set. Use measure-zero exceptions only when the exceptional set is proved null, such as a **nonconstant** split plane. Source-space partition conventions never change the closed-segment rule for a physical query. Exact equality in a row that vanishes identically on a block of receivers cannot be discarded as a rare sample.

## Global theorem including the actual grid output

**PROVEN.** Suppose that for each cell i the kernel has nonnegative credits lᵢ and oᵢ with

    lᵢ ≤ μ(F∩cellᵢ),       oᵢ ≤ μ(cellᵢ ∖ F).

These inequalities are the minimal accounting interface used by this proof. Disjoint proven regions are one sufficient implementation. Credits need not exhaust the world. Set k=128⁻³, L=Σlᵢ, U₋ᵢ=Σⱼ≠ᵢ(k−oⱼ). Require L>0. For lᵢ>0 define aᵢ=lᵢ/(lᵢ+U₋ᵢ); set aᵢ=0 otherwise. Let p be the **decoded** finite, nonnegative, exactly normalized released probability, with missing cells interpreted as zero. Then

    TV(p,q) ≤ 1 − Σᵢ min(pᵢ,aᵢ).

Indeed the true mass vᵢ is at least lᵢ, and the true mass outside i is at most U₋ᵢ. The fraction x/(x+y) increases with x and decreases with y, so qᵢ≥aᵢ. For normalized distributions TV(p,q)=1−Σ min(pᵢ,qᵢ). Substitution proves the claim. The different componentwise lower bounds need not be simultaneously tight. Only cells with positive p must be visited to evaluate the sum. An independently proved smaller upper bound on mass outside i can tighten the same theorem.

If the program returns CERTIFIED only when this exact bound, or a rigorously larger number, meets the requested tolerance, the bound holds **for every valid input and every search schedule on which it returns CERTIFIED**. A run that establishes no positive lower volume abstains, including when the true feasible volume is positive but undiscovered. Confining all feasible mass to one grid cell can certify its grid probability as one even when the continuous volume within the cell is largely unknown.

**Limits.** This theorem supplies conditional soundness and safe interruption. It promises neither exact recovery of F nor timely certification. It does not establish that every possible schedule eventually certifies. Budgeted termination requires a bounded scheduler and bounded or interruptible primitives, or an outer job policy that publishes nothing on failure. Neither a universal runtime nor a throughput comparison follows from the proof.
