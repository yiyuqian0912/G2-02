# What a mixed local interface can certify about a continuous source

Independent research handoff, 18 November 2026. The 54 archived v3 tasks cover nine polyhedral families and all three window sizes. A compact deterministic source constraint exists on some regions of these real problems. On five selected source cells the method solved the complete observation exactly. On 49 it abstained. Keep Tau-pencil and FastLabel as the existing paths. The implementation and measured records live in [experiments/mixed_patch](../experiments/mixed_patch/).

## What is identifiable from the observation

At finite resolution, each transition constrains a family of possible separating surfaces. Even a perfectly separating plane for the observations needs a physical reason to contain every compatible source. An oriented digital-plane preimage is appropriate *conditional on* a common oriented sheet. In a homogeneous LP the zero coefficient vector must be excluded, for example by normalizing a known nonzero separation or using signed coordinate charts. Allowing a different sheet for each edge and demanding one sheet for the whole fitted component exchange existential and universal quantifiers. Testing the generated source only can refute a proposed constraint; passing that test cannot establish containment of the feasible set. The existing v3 finding that 67 of 118 fitted components fail even at the generated source is already sufficient to rule out unconditional hard pruning with those fits. [Digital plane preimage implementation](https://tc18.liris.cnrs.fr/DataSets/Code/Preimage/preimage.html) · [Measured component failures](LOCALFIRST_AUDIT.md)

There is a restricted local exclusion rule that requires **no** plane fit. If one convex obstacle explains a set of inactive samples D for a given source, its shadow is convex and extends downstream on each source ray. Thus an active ray cannot pass through the convex hull of D on its way to that active receiver. This is a necessary condition, including for nonplanar silhouettes. It does not require the intervening receiver edges to be free. If several obstacles are allowed, the rule applies per common blocker. It is unsafe to assume a common blocker from proximity.

For an exact small example take inactive points (20,−20,−20), (20,20,−20), (20,0,20) and active point (30,0,0). For sources with x in [−10,0] and y,z in [−1,1], the active ray meets the inactive triangle at x=20, since both transverse coordinates there are bounded by 1/3. This rules out a **positive-volume** source box for the one-blocker explanation. Two closed boxes, [9,11]×[−12,12]×[−12,−8] and [9,11]×[−2,2]×[8,12], produce those very labels from the origin. The coordinates can be translated and uniformly scaled into the unit world. Combining explanations for different obstacles by taking the hull of all inactive points would lose a feasible source. This is a restricted result for four labels, not a population impossibility theorem. The classical visual hull is also an outer construction; its known viewpoints and silhouettes differ from the unknown source and hidden geometry here. [Laurentini, *The visual hull concept for silhouette-based image understanding*](https://doi.org/10.1109/34.273735)

A separate issue is normalization. Two full-dimensional boxes on opposite sides of z=0 can shadow two different FREE receivers at z=0 by closed contact, with a third receiver active. In a neighborhood of the witness, a positive source z misses one box and a negative z misses the other. The local feasible set then has zero 3D volume even though a source exists. A positive-volume lower certificate, or strict interior crossings for every inactive observation and strict separation for every active one at a legal interior source, suffices. An almost-sure argument under an ideal continuous generator differs from a per-artifact witness. Exact fixtures for both examples are in [the tests](../experiments/mixed_patch/test_certificates.py).

## Certificate over one source box and one receiver block

Let Q be a closed axis-aligned box of source positions and W a box containing the receiver **centres**. Decode the canonical binary64 geometry to exact rational facet rows. Each row f is affine, oriented so that f≤0 is inside. The research code scales rows by positive integers and uses arbitrary-precision products.

For one closed convex body and a source s outside it, clip the segment to x against all its facets. On a chart where no facet vanishes at s, a front row f has f(s)>0 and a back row b has b(s)<0. Intersection is equivalent to the simultaneous conditions

    f(x) ≤ 0                    for all front rows
    f(s)b(x) − b(s)f(x) ≤ 0     for all front/back pairs.

The first condition says every entering crossing is reached by the receiver. The second orders each entering crossing before every exiting crossing, by eliminating the segment parameter. If b(x)≤0 the back row imposes no finite exit and the inequality follows automatically once f(x)≤0. Equality is physical contact. Uncertain source aspect or receiver gating can result in abstention. A strictly violated necessary condition can exclude a body even when other facets change aspect.

For all s∈Q and x∈W, the determinant extrema occur at corner pairs because the form is affine in each argument separately. Box ranges suffice to exclude other bodies or certify a constant hit. When exactly one body can block, exactly one unresolved front/back pair remains, and f(x)<0 throughout W, its **complete** decision is

    inactive ⇔ t(s) ≤ t(x),     t(p) = b(p)/f(p).

The level planes share the intersection line of the two facets when the planes meet. The algebra also handles redundant/parallel rows without treating their number as geometric rank. Two observed extremizers now give the **exact** constraint for every FREE sample in the block:

    max over active x of t(x) < t(s) ≤ min over inactive x of t(x).

For a pure class only the appropriate one-sided bound is needed. Since f(s)>0, both bounds are affine half-spaces in s. The two stored witnesses are extrema of the *entire* labelled block. The proof keeps all other inequalities and bodies accounted for on the whole product. There is no H1 free-edge assumption, fit tolerance or assignment from the hidden source. If several possible blockers or determinant signs survive, the mixed block remains unresolved. Compatibility with one blocked endpoint alone is insufficient.

Intersect bands only when their proofs cover the **same** source box. Unknown blocks impose no cut. For a partly solved window this gives an outer volume only. For a completely covered window, legality is also required. The whole-window pilot proves separation from each component by a facet whose minimum signed gap on Q exceeds the clearance radius times its normal norm (squared integer comparison); this is sufficient and may abstain. Window/world boundary contacts of measure zero are handled only as such for volume. In one band we integrate an affine half-space by clipped-simplex inclusion/exclusion. For several bands a second implementation intersects exact triples of planes, validates every vertex against every constraint, and triangulates the supporting faces with rational determinants. Separately, axis-line interior points of each FREE class can be removed before taking extrema. On a line where the denominator has fixed sign a linear-fractional value lies between its endpoint values; successive removals preserve extrema even for disconnected sample masks.

## Measurements on the frozen canary

The [protocol](../experiments/mixed_patch/PROTOCOL.md) defined four rank-spaced aligned 8³ mixed patches per task where available. In total 206 distinct task/patch pairs had an internal directly adjacent opposite-response FREE pair. For each patch we tried an independently keyed random output cell, the archived highest-lower-mass cell, and the latter's 1/16 parent. The two reference-informed boxes measure conditional opportunities **near known support**. These locations cannot be used as an initial search algorithm or as evidence of whole-world coverage. The experiment did not load generated source coordinates.

| Source box (side in unit world) | Exact pencil | Constant response proves mixed patch impossible | Unknown | Tasks with ≥1 pencil |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Uniform 1/128 | 1 | 172 | 33 | 1/54 |
| Reference-cell parent, 1/16 | 38 | 0 | 168 | 17/54 |
| Reference cell, 1/128 | 133 | 0 | 73 | 43/54 |

On the applicable fine-cell patches the median was 512 FREE samples, 99 pure octree items, and two extremal observations. The median retained volume of an individual patch was 1.23% of Q. Across all 54 fine cells, including abstentions, the median after intersecting available patch constraints was **0.410% of Q**. Across all parents it was **100%**. Twelve fine cells had proved bands from at least two distinct facet pairs. In task `000019_w10_s3`, the strongest single patch retained 0.2701%; four patches using three different pairs together retained **0.00004915%**. The archived lower mass in the same cell was much smaller still. This is evidence for combining uncertain interfaces into a narrow region without assigning a point source or assuming three independent planes.

Fine-cell pencil counts by family were barriers 18/24, convex polyhedra 6/21, corridors 14/24, multiscale convex 14/21, occluder clusters 15/22, prisms 13/24, rotated boxes 21/24, thin slabs 15/22 and wedges 17/24. By window size they were 50/65, 40/71 and 43/70 for sides 64, 128 and 256. Counts share tasks and scenes. They are diagnostic coverage on this selected support, not independent population estimates. Exact primal segment clipping agreed on **43,086/43,086** real source–receiver queries from 43 tasks, including the per-patch conjunction. This checks the code; continuous validity comes from the inequalities.

The patch pilot took 8.67 s total task wall time on one Ryzen 7 5800H CPU thread, including decoding, mass-based cell selection, validation and the timing diagnostic. Chart screening over the 618 products accounted for 0.47 s. On 133 fine-cell charts, nine point tests by direct per-receiver evaluation with early exit took 0.188 s in total; compiling the two rational extrema and then querying nine times took 0.208 s. Both sides already knew the chart. This comparison gives no incentive to compile for a handful of isolated point queries. ## Can it certify the entire observation?

A follow-up independently processed the **whole** observation in the same preselected fine source cell. Up to two receiver subdivisions are permitted (≤73 box tests), and at most eight distinct face pairs can be integrated. Unsupported cases abstain. **Five of 54 cells** had a complete exact feasible volume under all the window observations and the legal-domain check. The other 49 consumed 3.53 s of unsuccessful first-pass work in total. Three window sizes are represented among the successes. Each of the five rational volumes is strictly positive. This proves a continuum of legal sources reproduces the entire finite observation in each of those five real tasks, even with the full geometry fixed. This proof needs neither the generated source nor an additional source pool. It gives no lower bound on the distance between two witnesses beyond what the feasible region itself supports. These are unnormalized volumes inside individual cells. Normalization still requires all relevant cells of the source world.

## Independent numerical audit

All five matched Tau-pencil outputs had equal binary64 lower/upper endpoints. All five slightly differed from the rational volume. The largest absolute difference was **6.77 × 10⁻¹⁴ of one cell**. There is also a check independent of our geometry code: in two of the 54 archived reference labels, sums of stored per-cell lower volumes exceed the volume of the entire cell. Exactly summing binary64 values as dyadic rationals gives 11,551 such cells out of 241,141 distinct task/cell records. The largest excess was **2.84 × 10⁻¹⁴ of one cell**. In a third task, `000059_w01_s3`, the old lower fraction exceeded the four-patch rational upper fraction by **1.05 × 10⁻¹² of one cell**. These magnitudes are tiny. They establish a literal enclosure failure for raw volume records; they do not measure a violation of the campaign's 0.05 TV target. Production implications require an error budget propagated through normalization, especially for tiny total feasible volumes. Silently clamping to cell capacity would not establish that budget. [Archive-only reproducer](../experiments/mixed_patch/audit_cell_volumes.py) · [Input digests and excesses](../experiments/mixed_patch/capacity_v1.json)

The smallest standalone reproduction needs one stored float. In `000049_w03_s0_tp.npz`, cell 1678663 has one lower-volume record, `0x1.0000000000080p-21`. The entire cell has volume `0x1.0000000000000p-21`. No visibility or summation algorithm is involved in this example.

There is a different quantifier issue in FastLabel's existing numerical audit. For measurable F⊆B of positive finite volume the continuous identity TV(U_F,U_B)=1−|F|/|B| is correct. Grid aggregation gives an **inequality**. The adaptive binomial design additionally needs the stipulated sampling and membership law conditional on the prior history, with failure budgets covering every route to acceptance. A floating-point sampler has finite support, so its continuous TV distance to Lebesgue-uniform on a nonzero-volume body is **one**, regardless of empirical agreement in tetrahedron volumes. The bound actually needed for the pass/fail test is on the **probability of the membership event**, or on a precisely defined discrete law with an additional comparison to the continuous target. A certified coupling plus a bound on the probability of the boundary strip would be one way to obtain it. This is a gap in a proposed justification, not a measured large bias in the 54 labels. The archived certificate audit also lists A1 (float pruning) and A2 (float bounds on pieces). Passing independent point comparisons cannot validate either globally. [FastLabel audit, conditions C1–C5](../experiments/fastlabel/CERTIFICATE_AUDIT.md)

## Decision and experiment for collaboration

Use these exact cases as independent regression fixtures and keep the mixed-patch solver experimental. The hook worth testing is a **partial proof**: when a mixed block has been discharged over a source region and contributes its cuts, retain those cuts on descendants and hand **only the unresolved receiver work** to the existing exact solver. Every descendant lies inside its proof domain. An incomplete proof still needs the untouched source-legality checks and the residual constraints. Run a matched ablation from the actual stage-1/outer roots, including failed screening, against unchanged Tau-pencil on disjoint held-out scenes. Measure full label wall time, upper/lower mass enclosures and missed/duplicate constraints. The present 49 abstentions provide a reason to test partial reuse, not to enable a second mandatory pass.

For spheres, the relevant constraint is quadratic. With free endpoints outside a sphere of center c and radius a, let d=x−s and u=c−s. For d≠0, closed-segment contact requires 0<d·u<d·d and |d×u|²≤a²|d|². A finite transition gives inequalities of this kind under a validated explanation. A single rational facet pencil and its two receiver extrema do not transfer automatically. Authoritative voxel physics also changes the world and its target. Neither branch supplies a curved or voxel posterior certificate to this pilot.

The ten new exact/algorithm tests and fifteen existing CPU regression/Local-first cases passed (25 total). Four GPU test variants were deselected. The [reproduction instructions](../experiments/mixed_patch/README.md) identify every input and output. No global posterior was replaced and no generated source position is an input to the prototype.
