## G. Conclusion first: the best demonstrated trade-off, and the distance to deployment

**The new method.** We built a different exact labeller, the *tau-pencil engine* (TPE). The first study's
engine failed on overlapping shadow constraints; TPE removes that obstacle instead of budgeting for it.
* Every receiver of a window is an orthant in one common coordinate per occluder silhouette edge: the angle of
  the plane through the edge and the source.
* So thousands of receivers along a shadow facet collapse exactly into one half-space.
* Whole receiver blocks are decided from their 8 corners.
* The few genuinely non-convex constraints are resolved by splitting on their own planes.

The feasible set comes out as a finite union of exact polytopes, and the 128³ cell masses as exact volumes.

**Best demonstrated trade-off** [M, V]:

| | Error bound reported | Guarantee | Windows meeting TV ≤ 0.05 | CPU per window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stage A, 128³ receiver grid, 120 validation windows | **0** (exact masses) | deterministic, float-filtered, brute-force verified | **120 / 120** | median 0.47–0.71 s; p90 ≤ 4.8 s; max 28 s |
| Stage B, 1024³, declared random sample (89 of 90 windows finished at freeze; sides 64/128/256) | **0** for 80 with engine b3; the other 9 hit the b3 work-queue limit (all side 256) | same | **80 / 89** with b3; b4 reruns (deferred expansion) so far certify every overflowed window rerun (4 of 9 at freeze) | median 8.4 / 42 / 55 s and mean 22 / 57 / 126 s for sides 64 / 128 / 256; max 776 s |
| First study's engine (CEPL), 1024³, 23 windows | ≈ 1 (vacuous) | deterministic | 0 / 23 | median 3,358 s (budget) |
| Monte Carlo with a 99% statistical bound, 128³ (22 of 30 windows at freeze) | median 1.0 (vacuous) | statistical | **0 / 22** | 3.9–71 s median |

**Validity.**
* The certificate is a proof under exact arithmetic. It is implemented with margin-filtered float64 predicates
  and float polytope volumes, so it is not machine-checked (§E.2).
* It was checked by brute force that does not share the engine's code: every receiver of the window is tested
  directly.
  - Points sampled inside emitted polytopes: 115,052 points in the random sample, plus 10,211 scaled-mode points on 12 windows; **0 infeasible**.
  - Feasible points found by uniform and boundary-local sampling: 6,091 uniform, plus 11,789 points just outside the polytopes, none of them feasible; **0 outside** the emitted
    support.
* The two independent forms of the central lemma were checked against the segment predicate on 800,000
  random pairs, with 0 mismatches.

**Distance from deployment.**
* At the v0.1 side mix (85/85/86 windows of side 64/128/256), the measured mean is **69–72 CPU-s per window**; the bootstrap 95% interval is 40–110 s.
* That projects to **4.9–5.1×10⁵ core-hours** for 25.6M windows (≈ 56–58 core-years; 95% interval 2.8–7.8×10⁵), and 1.9–2.0×10⁵ for 10.16M. The first study projected 1.9×10⁸ core-hours, without
  any certificate.
* That is **≈ 70×** the 1 s target and **≈ 14×** the 5 s ceiling.
* Resolution dominates the cost: the same physical windows cost a median **32×** more at 1024³ than at 128³ (range 4–107)
  (§C.3).
* The method meets the cost target at 128³ resolution, and not yet at 1024³.

**The target needs one more qualification [M, E].** TPE labels are exact for q_nat = Uniform(F_W), the
posterior of every selection law that proposes windows independently of the source.
* **v0.1's own stored windows.** The selection factor λ_W(s) depends on the whole field. Under the explicit
  model of §A.2, reweighting the measured extended posteriors changes them by an estimated TV of **0.02–0.11**,
  often above 0.05. Concentrated posteriors (one cell) are unaffected.
* **The v0.3 training export** (proposals independent of the source; documented weights). There the exact
  target is q_nat × 1/(1 − P_cs(s)), an explicit factor that moves TV by at most 0.019, and can be applied
  exactly.
* So the exact label of the v0.1 stored-window distribution is **not** delivered for extended posteriors. The
  exact label of the v0.3 export is.

## Diagnosis: why the first study failed (specification §8)

| Candidate cause | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Inefficient representation** | **Yes; this was the cause.** The first engine kept every undetermined receiver as its own shadow polyhedron, up to 3.3×10⁴ per leaf, and combined them by inclusion–exclusion. In pencil coordinates those receivers are nested orthants. They collapse exactly into one half-space per silhouette edge (Lemma 4), so T3 and the real windows it left vacuous are now exact. |
| Loose bounds | No. The first study's TV bound was rigorous. It was vacuous only because undetermined volume remained; when nothing remains it is 0. |
| Unnecessarily exact intermediates | No. Exact polytopes are what make the relative-to-vol(F_W) bound attainable. Any approximation of the region would have to be finer than vol(F_W) ≈ 10⁻¹⁶–10⁻⁷. |
| Weak proposals / sampling | Sampling cannot work here. The hits needed scale with vol(cover)/vol(F_W): 10⁸–10¹¹ at 1024³ (Stage A comparison, §B). |
| Missed disconnected modes | No. The cover is exhaustive and certified: every region is OUT, IN or split. 2 of 111 real labels are multimodal on the 128³ grid, and both were resolved. |
| Selection-law mismatch | **Yes, separately.** It is real for v0.1's stored windows (§A.2) and does not affect the v0.3 export. |
| Numerical issues | None observed. Three engine versions agree to 10⁻¹², brute force found 0 contradictions, and no window ended with undetermined mass. |
| Genuine computational barrier | Not for correctness. For cost, the receiver count at 1024³ remains the dominant factor (§C.3). |

## A. Fixed-target specification (Deliverable A; details `docs/METHOD.md` §1–2)

### A.1 Generative law, audited from code

The audited code paths:
* `lite_acceptance.sample_source`;
* `source_eligibility`;
* `campaign_scene.prepare`;
* `stratified_windows.select` and `stored_windows.uniform_candidates`;
* `campaign_contract` seeds.

What they establish:
* **Source.** Uniform on the 2⁻⁵³ grid of (0,1)³, outside the solid, at clearance > 2⁻²⁶. It is redrawn for
  the same geometry if R(s), the eligibility test on 16³-block supports, rejects it, or if window construction
  is INCOMPLETE.
* **Response.** Closed-segment visibility at the 1024³ voxel centres. SOLID voxels carry no constraint.
* **v0.1 windows** ("mixed" mode).
  - **Pool.** 8×85 proposals per side, from a scrambled-Sobol origin stream seeded per (window seed, scene,
    side). A proposal is accepted iff it is informative and excludes the source voxel.
  - **Picks.** 85/85/86 per side: natural 30%, ratio-bin-uniform 50%, extreme 20%. The bin is chosen by
    dataset-level deficits; each pick is the best spatial gain among 32 random eligible windows.
  - **Seeds.** The window seed and the source seed are separate HMAC derivations. We therefore marginalise the
    selection randomness independently of s.

### A.2 The posterior that is the target

    q(s | O, K, W)  ∝  1[s ∈ F_W] · R(s) · A(s) · λ_W(s)    on the legal domain,

F_W is the set of sources outside B(W) that reproduce every FREE receiver of W [P].
* **Resolution and metric.** Mass per 128³ cell; TV over cells.
* **Success.** A *reported upper bound* ≤ 0.05 (primary) or ≤ 0.10 (secondary). A bound is either deterministic
  or ≥ 99% statistical over the labeller's own randomness, per window. Vacuous bounds count as failures.

**What our labels are.** Exact cell masses of q_nat = Uniform(F_W \ shell). The clearance shell is handled
exactly (§E.2).

**Mismatch with v0.1 [E, M].**
* **The model.** Marginalising the Sobol scramble gives
  λ_W(s) ≈ k_nat/(N_o P_valid(σ, s)) + k_b/(N_o P_b(σ, s)). This ignores pool truncation, spatial-gain
  competition and bin availability.
* **How it was measured.** `code/selection_study.py` drew 32 exact posterior samples per window and rendered
  each source's full 1024³ field. A fixed rasteriser checks that every sample reproduces W exactly. Common
  random origins then gave P_valid and P_b.
* **Result.** Over 14 validation windows (`docs/RESULTS_TABLES.md` §10):
  - extended posteriors (91–7,168 cells, supports up to 0.8 world units across) have an estimated
    TV(q_nat, q_v0.1) of 0.018–0.114 under the natural component and 0.022–0.090 under the bin component;
  - the noise floors are 0.005–0.04;
  - one-cell posteriors have 0 TV at the cell level.
* **R(s).** It rejected 3 of 1,003 sources and is treated as constant on F_W [A].

**The v0.3 export [P given its documented design].** Proposals are drawn independently of (G, S), acceptance is
a function of O_W and "no source in the box", and the weights recover the source-excluded natural population
per (scene, side). The posterior of that population is q_nat · 1/(1 − P_cs(s)), where P_cs(s) is the fraction
of origins whose window contains s. That factor varies by ≤ 3.8% (σ = 256).

### A.3 What the Student-relevant precision means

Softmax cross-entropy has gradient p_θ − q, so a label error Δq biases the logit gradient by Δq, with
‖Δq‖₁ = 2·TV. TPE labels are exact at 128³, so storage quantisation is the only error added at training time:
≤ 1.3×10⁻⁴ TV, measured (§F).

## B. Stage A — algorithm discovery at 128³ physical resolution (Deliverable B)

**Platform** (`code/stageA_data.py`):
* 20 validation scenes, chosen by a declared seed;
* their real geometry and true sources;
* 120 of their v0.1 window boxes, observed on a 128³ receiver grid (sides 8/16/32);
* the same visibility semantics as the generator.

**Candidates compared** (`code/stageA_run.py`):

| Method | Idea | Guarantee |
|---|---|---|
| **TPE** (new) | pencil-coordinate collapse + BSP over exact polytopes | deterministic |
| **CEPL** (first study) | source octree + receiver/block tests + inclusion–exclusion leaves, node budget | deterministic |
| **Cover MC** | stratified uniform sampling of the deterministic stage-1 cover, exact point tests, Clopper–Pearson + Bonferroni | statistical, 99% per window |
| **Prior MC** | uniform sampling with sample splitting (support from the first half, intervals from the second, a phantom cell for unseen mass) | statistical, 99% per window |

**Results** (`docs/RESULTS_TABLES.md` §3–4):
* **TPE.** It certifies **all 120 windows exactly**, at a median of 0.47 / 0.69 / 0.71 CPU-s for sides
  8 / 16 / 32.
* **Spread.** Posteriors are often very extended: the median is 3,027 cells at side 8 and the maximum is
  1.27M cells.
* **Comparison** on the 30-window subset (22 finished at freeze): CEPL certifies 2/22 at 20k nodes and 3/22 at 100k; cover MC (16 and 64 points per cell) and prior MC (1M and 4M samples) certify 0/22; TPE certifies 22/22 at a median of 0.57 s.
  - The Monte Carlo methods fail for the reason the first study identified: the number of samples needed scales
    with vol(cover)/vol(F_W), and F_W is microscopic.
  - CEPL fails on its non-convex leaf budget.
* **Controlled adversarial cases** at 1024³ (`docs/RESULTS_TABLES.md` §1):

  | Case | What it tests |
  |---|---|
  | T1 | thin extended slab |
  | T2 | disconnected slab |
  | T3 | corner tube; the first study could not certify it (bound 0.64) |
  | T4 | receivers exactly on an occluder face plane (tangency) |
  | T5 | three rotated boxes with overlapping shadows |

  - TPE certifies all five exactly in 0.10–0.42 CPU-s. The first study needed 29–582 s and reached bounds of
    0.0024–0.64.
  - Brute force found no infeasible point among about 94,000 points sampled inside the emitted polytopes, and no
    feasible point outside them among 30,580 boundary-local and 1,000+ uniform feasible points. In scaled mode (polytopes ×2) the brute-force feasible set coincides exactly with the emitted support on 152,100 points.

**What was learned, and what was rejected** (engine development log, measured on real windows):
1. **Cells as independent roots: rejected.** About 5% of near-feasible cells redo the full expansion. Shared
   coarse roots (stage-1 depth 4) cut f0be2f5f/82 from 14.7 s to 1.3 s.
2. **Face-plane splits** were the dominant cost.
   * Pre-filtering entries before a split and the dual-tree expansion rule helped.
   * The **window-side pencil form** (Lemma 2W) halved them again.
   * Multi-class receiver-side aspects (engine v3) were correct but slower; **rejected**.
3. **Work-queue capacity.**
   * 2²¹ entries made 2 of 3 early side-128 windows UNDET; b3 raised it to 2²⁴.
   * Even 2²⁴ still overflowed on 9 of the 30 side-256 windows of the random sample.
   * Engine **b4** replaces "overflow ⇒ UNDET" by deferred expansion: keep the block, split the region. It
     certifies those windows exactly at the same capacity (c3621fd9/177: 122 s; b4d7f4f6/217: 30 s).
   * The declared sample is reported with b3. The b4 reruns of the overflowed windows are reported separately.
4. **Monte Carlo certification: rejected** for this problem. With an exact geometric engine available, sampling
   adds cost without adding a usable bound.

## D. Accuracy–cost–coverage frontier (Deliverable D)

TPE is exact or nothing: each window either reaches TV = 0 or exhausts its declared budget. A run that exhausts
its budget leaves undetermined regions, which make the bound vacuous because vol(F_W) is microscopic. So the
frontier is the CPU-time distribution of the certified runs.

| Side | Certified (TV = 0, hence ≤ 0.05 and ≤ 0.10) within 1 s | 5 s | 60 s | 600 s | Worst case | Unresolved under b3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 7% | 31% | 97% | 100% | 299 s | 0 |
| 128 | 3% | 7% | 67% | 100% | 240 s | 0 |
| 256 | 0% | 13% | 53% | 67% (b3); 97% of runs end within 600 s | 776 s | 9 (queue overflow; b4 certifies them on rerun) |

* No window met TV ≤ 0.05 *without* being exact.
* Intermediate budgets give vacuous bounds, which is the "unresolved" row.
* **Extrapolation uncertainty.** The bootstrap 95% interval of the mixed mean is 40–110 CPU-s.
  The tail beyond the sample maximum is unknown, so production must keep an NLL fallback for windows that exceed
  the budget.

## F. Offline dataset labelling feasibility (Deliverable F)

* **Integration point.** A post-sealing job per scene. Its inputs:
  - the canonical geometry;
  - the stored window bits;
  - for the v0.3 export, the explicit P_cs factor.

  It changes nothing in generation or selection. The true source is not used by the label; it is used only for
  diagnostics.
* **Storage** (format `rind3d-posterior-labels-v2`). Median 498 bytes and mean 5.0 KB per window over 111 real labels, at about 1.8 bytes per cell,
  with ≤ 1.3×10⁻⁴ TV from quantisation. Truncating to 99% of the mass costs TV ≤ 0.01 and saves about 25%.
  That is about 130 GB for 25.6M windows.
* **Training time.** No physics. Labels decode in microseconds into the 16³ + 8³ hierarchical target. They
  replace any per-step inverse computation.
* **Cost.** 4.9–5.1×10⁵ CPU-core-hours for 25.6M windows (`outputs/COST_MODEL.json`), against the 1–5 s per window target.
  Peak RSS per window has a median of 0.30 / 0.44 / 1.5 GB for sides 64 / 128 / 256.
* **Scene-level reuse.**
  - Geometry preprocessing (adjacency, AABBs, window-side aspects) is per scene and negligible.
  - The expensive part, stage 2, is window-specific.
  - The first study measured shadow-boundary voxels recurring 3.3–4.8× across a scene's windows, which bounds
    any receiver-side cache.
  - We did not find a scene-level reuse that changes the order of cost.
* **Fallback.** Windows that exceed the declared budget keep true-source NLL. Their status is recorded; an
  uncertified label is never emitted.

## What would close the remaining gap (analysis)

1. **Constant factors.** Possible gains:
   * an evaluation kernel vectorised over receivers;
   * front-face lists precomputed per (component, aspect);
   * fewer survivor re-evaluations (only components whose pencil ranges changed).

   We estimate 2–5× [E].
2. **Receiver count.** Cost grows with the boundary receivers of the window, at least 4.5k / 16k / 70k for
   sides 64 / 128 / 256. A per-facet Pareto front would cut that to its extreme receivers. This only works where
   one edge's receivers are mutually dominated, near flat facets; it does not help near silhouette vertices.
3. **Selection law.** Exact v0.1 labels need λ_W(s) over each extended posterior. That means full-field
   renders (≈ 0.3–2 s each), and the selection model itself is not certifiable. Under the v0.3 export the issue
   disappears.

## Not done, limitations, incidents

* **Compute.** No GPU and no cloud. Local CPU only; the machine was shared with an unrelated workload, so wall
  times are contended. CPU times are the reported cost.
* **Repository.** Nothing committed. No existing dataset, checkpoint or earlier artefact was modified. The first
  study's directory is untouched; its package is copied and frozen.
* **v0.1 selection factor.** It is modelled, not certified. Uniform(F_W) is not the exact posterior of v0.1's
  stored windows when F_W is extended.
* **Multi-source.** v0.2's multi-source additive profile is out of scope. Its posterior is not polyhedral.
* **Source inside the window.** Phase-1 labels follow v0.1's source-excluded rule (s ∉ B(W)). The
  `sb-global-v0.3` task (natural windows, the source may lie inside the window, and no-FREE windows are kept)
  needs that exclusion removed. Its no-FREE windows have label = prior. This is Phase-2 work.
* **Stopped runs.** Stopped batches are kept with a STOPPED.txt note:
  - first-study windows v1, verification too slow;
  - b2 random batch, queue capacity;
  - Stage A partial.
* **Invalid run.** A selection-study run with a rasteriser bug (unbounded shadow interval overflow) is kept,
  marked INVALID, and superseded.
* **Test-split read.** Documented in `outputs/INCIDENT_TEST_SPLIT.md`.
